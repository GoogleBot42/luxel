# coolaura
kind: 1D
sensors: no

## What it looks like
A calm, aquatic "aura" effect on a strip. Soft pulses of blue-green light appear at random places, swell up, spread outward, and fade away — like slow ripples or breathing patches of glow. Several pulses are usually alive at once and their light adds together where they overlap. On top of that, the whole strip's intensity slowly breathes in and out over several seconds, so the entire effect periodically dims toward its base color and blooms back up. Overall feel: slow, ambient, meditative; nothing moves fast.

Provenance note: the original was machine-generated by a node-graph-to-pattern compiler, so its structure is "stanzas" (pulse generator, global time envelope, gradient colorizer). Reimplement the behavior, not the structure.

## Algorithm
State kept between frames:
- A wall-clock accumulator in seconds (advanced by the frame delta).
- A small fixed pool of pulse slots (about ten). Each slot stores: alive flag, birth time, and a center position chosen uniformly at random along the strip (as a fraction of strip length).
- The time at which the next pulse is allowed to spawn.
- A per-pixel intensity buffer, length = pixel count (rebuilt every frame, so it need not persist, but the original keeps it as a frame-scope buffer).

Per frame (before rendering):
1. Clear the intensity buffer to zero.
2. Spawning: if the clock has passed the next-spawn time and a free slot exists, activate the first free slot with a fresh uniform-random center position and record its birth time. Schedule the next spawn for roughly one-and-a-half seconds later, jittered by a roughly bell-shaped random offset (the original sums three uniform randoms to approximate a normal distribution, scaled so the jitter is a modest fraction of the base interval, and can push the interval somewhat shorter or longer).
3. Pulse update: for each live pulse, compute its age. Pulses live a fixed lifetime of a few seconds (about four); past that they are freed. For a live pulse:
   - Temporal envelope: a half-sine over normalized age (zero at birth, peak at mid-life, zero at death).
   - Width: starts at roughly a tenth of the strip and grows linearly with age, ending up wider than the whole strip by end of life — so pulses visibly spread as they fade.
   - Spatial profile: across the pulse's current extent (clipped to the strip), a half-sine bump — zero at the edges, peak at the pulse center.
   - Add (temporal envelope × spatial profile) into the intensity buffer for every covered pixel. Contributions from overlapping pulses accumulate.
4. Global breathing envelope: a raised-cosine wave of the clock with a period of several seconds (around eight), ranging from zero to one. Multiply every pixel's accumulated intensity by this scalar.
5. Colorize: map each pixel's (envelope-scaled) intensity through a two-stop RGB gradient (below).

Per pixel (render): look up the precomputed RGB for that pixel and square each channel before output (a gamma-like curve that deepens the darks and makes the pulses feel softer).

Randomness: uniform random for pulse position; approximately-normal jitter (sum of uniforms) for spawn intervals.

Layout: pure 1D, indexed by pixel fraction of the strip; no hardcoded pixel count — scales to any strip length as-is. The pool size (~10 concurrent pulses) is a fixed constant; fine to keep fixed.

## Colors
A two-stop gradient over intensity:
- At zero intensity: a medium blue-cyan (no red, moderate green, full blue). Note this means the strip is never black — the "background" is this dim-ish blue, further dimmed by the channel-squaring and by the global breathing envelope. When the breathing envelope hits its low point, everything collapses toward the low-intensity end of the gradient.
- At full intensity: a green-teal (no red, strong green, weak blue).
So pulses read as green-teal blooms rising out of a blue field. Intensities can exceed the gradient's top stop when pulses overlap; the gradient clamps at its end stops.

## Controls
None. No sliders, toggles, or pickers.

## Timing
- New pulse roughly every second or two (jittered).
- Each pulse lives a few seconds (rise and fall symmetric).
- Whole-strip breathing cycle: several seconds (around eight).

## Non-obvious details / bugs in the original
- The original's live-pulse counter is buggy: the spawn logic checks one counter but increments/decrements a differently-named (implicitly created) variable, so the checked counter never changes. Net effect: the "max concurrent pulses" cap is enforced only by slot availability, which is equivalent — reimplement with a working slot pool and don't reproduce the bug.
- The original's per-frame pulse-update loop stops at the first dead slot instead of skipping it, so a pulse dying mid-pool can briefly suppress later pulses. This reads as a subtle glitch, not a feature — iterate all slots and skip dead ones.
- Channel-squaring at output is deliberate and matters to the look (softer, deeper).
