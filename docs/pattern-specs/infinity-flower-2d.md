# Infinity Flower 2D
kind: 2D
sensors: no

## What it looks like

A stylized flower drawn at the center of a 2D matrix: a glowing round center disc surrounded by radiating petals arranged like a sunflower's phyllotaxis. Every couple of seconds the flower "regenerates": the petals spin rapidly around the center while the center's glow fades out (about a second of transition), and when the spin ends a brand-new randomly generated "species" appears — different petal count, petal length and width, and a fresh color scheme — and holds steady for a second or two before the next regeneration. Endless novel flowers, hence the name. Requires a 2D mapped display.

## Algorithm

### Precomputed polar cache (clever bit #1)

Two per-pixel arrays (radius from center, and angle from center normalized to a positive full-turn range) are filled during the very first rendered frame by a special first-pass renderer; once the last pixel has been processed, the renderer function pointer is swapped to the normal renderer. All later frames read polar coordinates from the cache instead of recomputing them. Assumes the pixel map is static. (An implementer could equally precompute this at startup if map coordinates are accessible outside render.)

### Species generation (randomized)

Each new flower randomizes:
- **Petal count**: from two up to about a dozen.
- **Petal length** (max radius): somewhere between roughly a third and two-thirds of the half-display, so the flower comfortably fits.
- **Petal width factor**: a random multiplier spanning roughly a 1×–2× range around "medium".
- **Petal hue**: uniform random. **Center hue** = petal hue offset by the golden-ratio conjugate (clever bit #2: guarantees a pleasing contrasting color).
- **Center disc radius**: about a tenth of the display half-width, with a small random variation (two nearby sizes chosen by a biased coin flip).
- **Color-variant flag** (fair coin flip): when set, petal hue additionally shifts with radius, giving petals a gradient along their length instead of a flat color.
- A small random per-species hue offset applied only to every other petal (alternating petals get a slightly different tint).

Petal placement (clever bit #3): each petal's direction is separated from the previous by the **golden angle**, sunflower-style, generated by repeatedly rotating a point around the center by that angle. Each petal record stores its tip position (as a rotatable point), its angle, and its hue.

### Frame lifecycle (state machine via swapped per-frame function)

Two per-frame handlers, switched by reassigning a function reference:
- **Steady state**: accumulate elapsed milliseconds; after roughly a second and a half of display, switch to the transition handler and set the rotation step (nominally a coin flip on direction, but the comparison is against a huge threshold so it *always* spins the same way — a benign bug an implementer may fix by flipping fairly).
- **Transition**: each frame, rotate every petal's stored point by the fixed rotation step (about a radian **per frame** — note this is frame-rate dependent; a time-scaled rotation would be the robust fix) and recompute its angle from the rotated point. Simultaneously fade the center brightness linearly from full to zero across the transition (about a second). When the transition time elapses, generate the new species, restore full center brightness, and switch back to the steady handler.

### Per-pixel rendering

Using the cached radius/angle:
1. Outside the petal length: black.
2. Inside the center disc: center hue, full saturation, brightness = center's current fade level shaped by a smooth hump over the disc radius (bright core, soft edge).
3. Otherwise, test the pixel against each petal: compute the wrapped angular difference between pixel angle and petal angle. The petal's angular half-width at this radius = a base tolerance that **shrinks inversely with radius** (clever bit #4: compensates for fixed pixel pitch so petals keep a consistent drawn width instead of fanning out), multiplied by the species' width factor, and shaped along the radius by a smooth hump peaking mid-petal (this gives the leaf-like pointed-tip outline). If the angular difference is inside that width, the pixel is lit: brightness equals the angular difference (small but floored just above zero — so petal centerlines are dim and edges brighter, an outline-ish look), hue = petal hue (plus a radius-proportional shift if the color variant is active), and saturation is slightly reduced in proportion to brightness (brighter parts a touch pastel). First matching petal wins.
4. If no petal matched, the pixel gets brightness zero.

## Colors

Each species: one random petal hue (fully or near-fully saturated), with alternate petals subtly tinted, optionally graded along the petal length; center disc in a contrasting hue chosen by golden-ratio offset. Background black. Overall: vivid two-tone flowers on black, different every cycle.

## Controls

None.

## Timing

Each flower holds for a bit over a second, then a roughly one-second spin/fade transition, so a new species appears every couple of seconds. Spin speed is frame-rate dependent as written (fast blur at high FPS).
