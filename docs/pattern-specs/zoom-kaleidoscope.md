# zoom kaleidoscope
kind: 2D
sensors: no

## What it looks like
An endlessly zooming, kaleidoscopic interference lattice centered on the display: nested square-ish and diamond-ish ring shapes collide into a fractal moiré that appears to flow outward (or inward) forever. Bright filigree ribbons wander over a black field, colored by a slowly cycling rainbow, with optional gentle drifting of the whole center, optional slow rocking rotation, and optional pie-slice radial spokes for extra kaleidoscope symmetry. Motion feel: the ring lattice flows steadily and fairly briskly; hue drifts through the wheel over on the order of a minute; the noise-driven brightness texture shimmers continuously.

## Coordinate setup (per frame)
Uses the 2D transform stack. Each frame: reset transforms, recenter so the middle of the mapped area is the origin, then scale coordinates by a zoom factor from a slider (smaller factor = more magnified). If "move around" is enabled, also translate the center by a small wandering offset (roughly a third of the display at most) generated by sines of slowly-evolving perlin noise, so the vortex meanders organically. If "rotate" is enabled, rotate by a full turn multiplied by a triangle-shaped oscillation with a period around a minute — a slow rock back and forth rather than continuous spin.

## Per-frame state/phases
- A steadily advancing ring phase (a several-minute time base multiplied up so rings visibly flow).
- Two very slowly drifting axes used for noise sampling (multi-minute time bases stretched over a wide range).
- A global hue phase cycling on the order of a minute or two.
- Two per-pixel arrays persist between frames: last brightness and last hue, for temporal smoothing.

## Per-pixel work
1. Compute two distance metrics from the (transformed) point to the origin: Chebyshev distance (max of |x|,|y| — concentric square contours) and Manhattan distance (|x|+|y| — concentric diamond contours), the latter nudged by a small constant offset.
2. Subtract the shared advancing ring phase from each, then combine the two animated fields with a **bitwise XOR of their fixed-point representations** (Pixel Blaze numbers are 16.16 fixed point; its XOR operator acts on that raw representation). This XOR of two slightly different ring families is the whole trick: it yields a self-similar, fractal lattice, and advancing the common phase makes it read as an infinite zoom.
3. Fold the XOR result with a triangle wave to get a bounded field value.
4. Hue = half that field value plus the global hue phase, then per-pixel exponential smoothing toward the new hue with a fixed gentle blend (roughly a tenth per frame), and finally folded by a triangle wave so it wraps smoothly.
5. If the spoke count slider is nonzero: compute the angle around the center normalized to a full turn, and add a triangle wave of (angle × spoke count) to the field value. This happens after hue is computed, so spokes sculpt brightness only.
6. Brightness = ridged perlin noise (a few octaves, moderately increasing frequency and high persistence) sampled with the field value as one axis and the two slow-drift phases as the others — so the lattice is dressed in shimmering ridged-noise texture.
7. Per-pixel exponential smoothing of brightness with a slider-set coefficient, then a smoothstep contrast window whose low/high edges come from the contrast slider, then raise to about the fourth power for deep blacks. Full saturation.

## Colors
Full-saturation spectral hues over black; hue is derived from the pattern field plus a slow global rotation, so ribbons carry rainbow gradients that creep around the wheel. No fixed palette.

## Controls
- Toggle "rotate": enables the slow rocking rotation.
- Toggle "move around": enables the perlin wander of the center.
- Slider "extra geometry": quantized to a whole spoke count from none up to about eight radial spokes.
- Slider labelled like "zoom speed" (really zoom amount): higher = smaller scale factor = more magnified. A tiny floor keeps it from degenerate zero scale.
- Slider "smooth": at the top, the blend coefficient is tiny — heavy smearing/trails; at the bottom, near-instant response.
- Slider "contrast": squared response; higher narrows the smoothstep window (raising the low edge, lowering the high edge) for punchier, sparser highlights.

## Layout notes
Fully mapper-driven, resolution-independent; only assumes a 2D map roughly in the unit square. Two full-size scratch arrays sized by pixel count. No 1D/3D renderers — suggest a 1D fallback that feeds index-as-x if desired. One distance helper in the source references a "mean distance" constant tuned for a rectangular matrix, but that metric (and two other alternative metrics) are defined and unused — implement only Chebyshev and Manhattan.

## Non-obvious bits
- The fixed-point bitwise XOR of two distance fields is the load-bearing trick; with plain arithmetic you will not get the fractal lattice.
- Temporal smoothing is applied separately to hue (fixed, light) and brightness (user-set), which lets colors stay coherent while brightness trails are adjustable.
